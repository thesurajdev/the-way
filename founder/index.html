The Founder’s Framework

How I Choose to Live

The founder’s personal perspective, in the founder’s own words — not a list of requirements for anyone else.

First personPersonal view

This page is a personal belief, not a principle of The Way.

It is written in the first person on purpose. The founder can be wrong, can be criticized, and does not own another person’s conscience. The principles themselves live on the Principles page; the protections live in the Safeguards.

Introduction

The Way is the name I give to my personal approach to life. It is not a claim that I have discovered the correct way for everyone. It is not a request for people to believe me. It is not a demand that anyone adopt my worldview. I am simply putting into words how I currently choose to understand and live life. I may be wrong about some things. I may change my understanding. Future knowledge may change what I believe. Someone else may have a better idea. I want The Way to leave room for all of those possibilities. If another person finds something useful here, they may take it. If something does not fit their life, they can leave it. If they disagree, they are free to disagree. If they find a better way, I would rather see that better way followed than see my ideas followed blindly.

How I See the Individual

I see every person as an individual. No two people experience life in exactly the same way. We have different:

  • experiences
  • families
  • cultures
  • abilities
  • personalities
  • circumstances
  • interests
  • relationships
  • beliefs
  • dreams
  • limitations

Because of this, I do not believe that everyone needs to live the same kind of life. I do not need another person to become like me for me to respect them. And I do not need to become like another person to respect them. I believe individuality is something worth protecting.

How I See Belief

I believe belief belongs primarily to the individual. A person may believe in God. A person may believe in many gods. A person may believe in spirituality. A person may believe in something supernatural. A person may believe only in what can currently be observed or demonstrated. A person may be uncertain. A person may change their mind. I do not need to decide which of these possibilities another person must accept. For me, the important principle is that belief should not be forced. I also believe that something meaningful does not need to be publicly demonstrated. Someone may pray privately. Someone may worship privately. Someone may meditate privately. Someone may follow a personal ritual privately. Someone may share these practices publicly if they genuinely want to. I simply do not believe that a person needs an audience to prove the sincerity of their beliefs.

How I See Religion

I do not see religion as something that needs to be defeated. Religions are part of human history, culture, identity, community, art, philosophy, and personal experience. People may find genuine meaning in them. I may agree with some ideas and disagree with others. But disagreement does not require disrespect. I do not want The Way to become another system that says:

“Our way is true, therefore your way is wrong.” That would contradict what I am trying to create. A person may follow a religion and still find value in The Way. A person may follow no religion. A person may follow The Way. A person may follow several philosophical or spiritual traditions. A person may simply live without giving themselves any label. I believe people should have room to make that choice.

How I See Privacy

I believe some parts of life are deeply personal. Beliefs can be personal. Spiritual practices can be personal. Relationships can be personal. Thoughts can be personal. Doubts can be personal. Happiness can be personal. A person should not feel that everything meaningful in their life has to become public. I also recognize that people have different attitudes toward privacy. Some people enjoy sharing. Some people prefer privacy. Neither needs to be treated as superior. Privacy is an option, not a requirement.

How I See Happiness

I do not believe there is one universal definition of happiness. For one person, happiness may be family. For another, it may be travel. For another, knowledge. For another, art. For another, nature. For another, spirituality. For another, friendship. For another, building a business. For another, a quiet life. For another, something I cannot imagine. I do not need to understand another person’s source of happiness for it to be meaningful to them. I want to find what makes my own life meaningful while giving others the freedom to find theirs.

How I See Life

I want to experience life rather than spend my entire life preparing for some future existence that I cannot verify. I want to:

experience, explore, learn, create, love, connect, question, make mistakes, improve, and live. I do not need certainty about what happens after death in order to value life now. I may have questions about existence that I cannot answer. I can live with those questions. For me, uncertainty is preferable to pretending to know something I do not know.

How I See Death and What Comes After

I do not base my way of living on promises of heaven or threats of hell. I do not need an afterlife to give this life meaning. I recognize that other people may hold different beliefs about death and what may come afterward. Those beliefs can remain personal. For me, the life that I can actually experience and influence is enough reason to care about how I live.

How I See Personal Growth

I want to grow. But I do not believe growth has one universal definition. Growth can mean:

  • learning
  • becoming more thoughtful
  • becoming kinder
  • becoming healthier
  • developing skills
  • creating something
  • understanding myself
  • understanding others
  • becoming more independent
  • repairing mistakes
  • becoming more responsible
  • finding peace
  • becoming comfortable with a simpler life

I do not want to measure my life only through money, status, fame, or social approval. I want growth to have meaning beyond comparison.

How I See Other People

I want to remember that every person has a life that I cannot completely see. Someone may carry experiences I know nothing about. Someone may understand something I do not. Someone may struggle with something I find easy. Someone may find meaning in something I find meaningless. Therefore, I want to approach people with curiosity before judgment. Respect does not mean agreeing with everyone. It means remembering that the other person is a person.

How I See Freedom

I value personal freedom. I want the freedom to think. I want the freedom to question. I want the freedom to change my mind. I want the freedom to choose my beliefs. I want the freedom to choose my way of living within the applicable legal and social boundaries. I want others to have those freedoms too. But I do not understand freedom as unlimited permission to harm another person. My freedom exists alongside someone else’s freedom. Therefore, when my choices significantly affect others, I believe I should consider their rights, dignity, safety, and legitimate interests.

How I See Ethics

I do not want to create an eternal list of commandments and claim that every situation in every century must be judged by exactly the same answer. Life is complicated. Circumstances differ. Knowledge changes. Consequences differ. People have different needs. Therefore, I believe difficult decisions should be considered rather than answered through blind obedience. When I face an ethical question, I want to ask:

Who is affected? What are the consequences? Am I causing unnecessary harm? Am I respecting the other person’s dignity and legitimate rights? What does the situation require? What information do I actually have? Is there a better alternative? What effect could this have on nature and future generations? I may still make the wrong decision. The purpose is not to become morally perfect. The purpose is to think responsibly and remain willing to learn.

How I See Nature

I see humans as part of nature rather than completely separate from it. We depend upon the natural world. Air, water, soil, plants, animals, ecosystems and resources make human life possible. Therefore, I want to consider nature when I make choices. This does not mean that every environmental decision has a simple answer. Human needs matter too. Development matters. Livelihoods matter. Technology matters. But nature should not become invisible simply because humans need progress. I want human growth and care for nature to be considered together.

How I See Food and Lifestyle

I do not believe there needs to be one universal diet or lifestyle. People may eat differently because of:

  • preference
  • culture
  • religion
  • health
  • location
  • availability
  • economics
  • ethics
  • circumstance

The same applies to clothing, festivals, routines, entertainment, work, relationships and other personal choices. I do not want The Way to create unnecessary rules around ordinary life.

How I See Children

I believe children should have the opportunity to discover the world rather than being treated as extensions of their parents. Children should be able to learn about:

  • science
  • nature
  • history
  • cultures
  • religions
  • philosophy
  • ethics
  • different perspectives
  • critical thinking

Parents naturally share their own beliefs and traditions. I do not believe that sharing a belief is inherently wrong. What concerns me is coercion that prevents a developing person from ever questioning what they were given. As a child grows and develops the ability to reason independently, their ability to form and express their own worldview should also grow. I would personally prefer a society in which children are given substantial space to explore different ideas before being expected to identify permanently with one. The exact age, responsibilities, and legal boundaries surrounding this should remain matters for families, educators, child-development knowledge, and applicable law—not something The Way attempts to impose as law.

How I See Culture and Tradition

I value tradition when it has meaning. Traditions can connect people to:

  • family
  • history
  • community
  • art
  • memory
  • identity

I do not want to destroy traditions simply because they are old. But I also do not believe something becomes permanently correct simply because it is traditional. A tradition can be preserved. It can be changed. It can be combined with something new. It can be left behind. It can be rediscovered later. Each generation should have room to decide what remains meaningful.

How I See Society

I see society as a collection of individuals who need to coexist. Society requires cooperation. People need shared systems. We need education, infrastructure, public services, economic systems, institutions, rules, and ways to resolve disputes. But society does not need everyone to have identical private beliefs. A healthy society can contain people with different:

  • religions
  • philosophies
  • cultures
  • lifestyles
  • opinions
  • traditions

The goal is not to eliminate difference. The goal is to make coexistence possible.

How I See Government and Law

I consider government and law separate from my personal philosophy. The Way is about how I choose to live. Government and law are systems used to organize a country and protect the public order, rights, and interests defined through legitimate institutions. I do not claim that The Way should become the law. I do not believe my personal philosophy automatically gives me authority over other citizens. I can personally follow The Way while respecting the legal system in which I live. If I disagree with a law, that is a matter for lawful civic participation, discussion, and democratic or legal processes—not an automatic authority for me to impose my personal philosophy on everyone else.

How I See Disagreement

I expect disagreement. Someone may say:

“I think The Way is wrong.” I can accept that. Someone may say:

“I believe the opposite.” I can accept that too. Someone may find a flaw in something I wrote. I should consider it. Someone may discover a better idea. I should listen. I do not want agreement to become the measure of respect. People can disagree and still coexist.

How I See The Way Itself

The Way should never become more important than the people it was created to serve. I do not want:

  • unquestionable leaders
  • unquestionable scriptures
  • compulsory rituals
  • forced membership
  • punishment for leaving
  • pressure to recruit
  • financial exploitation
  • claims of supernatural authority
  • ownership of people’s consciences

If The Way ever becomes a system that prevents people from questioning it, something has gone wrong. If The Way ever becomes a reason to disrespect people who do not follow it, something has gone wrong. If The Way ever becomes more concerned with preserving itself than helping people live meaningful lives, something has gone wrong.

How I See Its Future

I cannot know what humanity will understand 100 years from now. The people of the future may know things I cannot imagine. They may solve problems I cannot solve. They may discover that some of my ideas were incomplete. They may reject some of them entirely. I want them to have that freedom. The Way should therefore be treated as a living philosophy. Not because it must constantly change. But because it must always remain capable of change.

My Personal Commitment

I want to try to live in a way that:

allows me to be myself,

allows others to be themselves,

respects personal belief and privacy,

does not require public performance of faith,

encourages learning and questioning,

accepts that I can be wrong,

respects human dignity,

considers nature,

recognizes responsibility alongside freedom,

and remains open to becoming better.

I do not expect to live these principles perfectly.

I will make mistakes.

I will sometimes contradict myself.

I will sometimes make decisions that I later understand differently.

That is part of being human.

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is to keep learning how to live better.

My Final Principle

If I had to reduce everything I have written to one thought, it would be this:

I want to find my own way of living, give every other person the same freedom to find theirs, respect the world we share, and keep learning how we can all live and grow better together. That is what I currently call:

The Way

Find your way. Respect theirs. Grow together.
